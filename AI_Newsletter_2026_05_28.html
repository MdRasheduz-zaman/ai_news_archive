Issue #33  |  Week of May 28, 2026

Hi, there! This week's theme is AI Enters the Scientific Method Itself. Google's Gemini for Science platform connects AI to 30+ life science databases and introduces multi-agent hypothesis generation and computational discovery — AI that participates in research, not just assists it. That shift arrives alongside Eli Lilly's $3.83B vaccine acquisitions, the FDA's AI-enabled clinical trial pilot, and a survey showing 80% of physicians are already using AI without formal training. A lot to unpack.

🚀 EVENT OF THE WEEK

Gemini for Science — Google Connects AI to 30+ Life Science Databases, Launches Hypothesis Generation

At Google I/O 2026, Google unveiled Gemini for Science — a suite of AI research tools that inserts AI directly into the scientific method rather than simply accelerating tasks around it.

The platform centres on Science Skills: a bundle integrating Google's agentic research platforms with over 30 major life science databases — including UniProt, the AlphaFold Database, AlphaGenome API, and InterPro. Complex structural bioinformatics and genomic analyses that previously required hours of manual tool-switching now execute in minutes through a single agentic interface.

Three experimental tools launched alongside:

Hypothesis Generation (Co-Scientist): A multi-agent system that generates, debates, and evaluates competing research hypotheses in parallel — an "idea tournament" where AI argues both sides of a scientific question before converging on the most defensible direction.

Computational Discovery (AlphaEvolve + ERA): An agentic research engine that generates and scores thousands of code variations in parallel. In genomics, AlphaEvolve improved DeepConsensus (DNA sequencing error correction) with a 30% reduction in variant detection errors.

Literature Insights (NotebookLM): AI-driven scientific literature synthesis in report, infographic, audio, and video formats.

Two validation papers published simultaneously in Nature on May 19. ERA outperformed the CDC's own COVID-19 hospitalization forecasting ensemble. Over 100 institutions are now collaborating on validation. Isomorphic Labs highlighted at I/O as using Gemini for Science in pre-clinical pipelines for immune disorders and cancer.

Why this matters: Every prior AI wave improved specific research tasks. Gemini for Science attempts something categorically different: AI that participates in the scientific method — from hypothesis to experiment design to literature synthesis. The question is no longer "how do AI tools help scientists?" but "what does the scientific method look like when AI is a participant rather than a tool?"

Key takeaways:

  • Science Skills integrates 30+ life science databases into a single agentic interface — structural bioinformatics and genomics in minutes
  • Co-Scientist introduces multi-agent hypothesis tournaments validated by 2 simultaneous Nature papers
  • ERA outperforms CDC's own COVID hospitalization forecasting ensemble — a peer-reviewed benchmark that is hard to argue with

📚 Top Research Papers

PulmoFoundation: A Clinically Validated Foundation Model for Comprehensive Lung Pathology

arXiv 2605.25878 | May 25, 2026

A subspecialty-specific lung pathology foundation model trained on ~40,000 H&E whole-slide images and prospectively validated across 32 clinical tasks in an RCT with 1,357 patients. Achieves 92.3% average performance; reduces repeat pathologist reviews by 44–84% depending on procedure type. Directly addresses the 94% RCT-gap critique — this is the clinical AI field's clearest demonstration that rigorous prospective validation is achievable and changes the evidentiary standard for "clinical-grade."

Pathology AI Clinical Validation

Healthcare AI GYM for Medical Agents

arXiv 2605.02943 | May 2026

The first gymnasium-compatible multi-turn RL environment for training generalizable clinical AI agents — spanning 10 domains, 3,600+ tasks, 135 domain-specific tools, and 828,000 medical passages. Identifies "agentic collapse" in vanilla GRPO training and proposes TT-OPD, achieving +3.9pp improvement on 10 of 18 benchmarks with better multi-turn tool utilization. Provides the missing training infrastructure for agents that can handle realistic multi-step clinical reasoning.

Clinical Agents Reinforcement Learning

AgentRx: LLM Agents for Multimodal Clinical Prediction Tasks

arXiv 2605.10286 | May 2026

A systematic benchmark evaluating LLM agents for clinical prediction using multimodal real-world hospital data — EHR, imaging, radiology reports, and clinical notes. Key finding: single-agent frameworks substantially outperform multi-agent systems. Naive multi-agent collaboration actively degrades performance on multimodal clinical tasks. Challenges the assumption that federated multi-agent clinical AI can substitute for centralized approaches — critical given that hospital privacy constraints often mandate federated designs.

Clinical AI Federated Learning

Trends in AI and Human-AI Interaction in Clinical Trials

arXiv 2605.29096 | May 2026

A hybrid human-GPT-5.5 analysis of clinical trial registry data mapping temporal trends in AI terminology and geographic distribution of AI trials. China and the US lead in AI trial volume; Europe is catching up. Human-AI agreement varies significantly by task complexity. First systematic temporal map of AI's infiltration into clinical trial design — and a demonstration of hybrid human-AI methodology's scalability for clinical registry analysis.

Medical Open Network for AI — the de-facto PyTorch framework for medical image analysis. Scanpy

⭐ ~2,000 stars | scverse / Theis Lab | Python

The standard single-cell RNA-seq analysis toolkit in Python — preprocessing, dimensionality reduction, clustering, trajectory inference, and differential expression at scale. As AI drug candidates increasingly require single-cell transcriptomic profiling for target validation and mechanism-of-action elucidation, Scanpy connects raw sequencing data to biological insight.

ESM — Evolutionary Scale Modeling

⭐ ~4,000 stars | Meta FAIR | Python

Meta's protein language model family — ESM-2 (pre-trained on 250M+ proteins) and ESMFold (single-sequence structure prediction at AlphaFold2 accuracy, 60× faster). ESM-2 embeddings are now standard for protein engineering, binding prediction, and antibody CDR design. ESMFold's speed makes it the go-to for large-scale protein design screening runs where AlphaFold inference cost is prohibitive.

REINVENT4

⭐ ~500 stars | AstraZeneca / MolecularAI | Python

AstraZeneca's open-source molecular AI platform for de novo drug design, scaffold decoration, R-group replacement, linker design, and library design. Combines reinforcement learning with generative chemistry models (RNN, Transformer, MolFormer) and a multi-objective scoring framework integrating docking, ADMET prediction, and synthesizability filters — one of the few drug design platforms with a full RL loop and industry-grade multi-objective optimization.

ChemBERTa

⭐ ~800 stars | Seyone Chithrananda | Python

BERT-style transformer pre-trained on 77M SMILES strings for molecular property prediction — ADMET, bioactivity classification, and toxicity screening. Fine-tunes on small labeled datasets with strong performance, making it accessible for academic labs running property-prediction pipelines without large chemical datasets. The lightweight transformer alternative to graph neural network approaches in molecular ML.

⚠️ AI Criticism & Concerns

Critical Perspectives on AI Ethics and Safety

Gemini for Science and the Discovery Attribution Problem — Who Owns an AI-Generated Hypothesis?

When an AI system drawing on 30+ curated life science databases proposes a novel drug target hypothesis — and that hypothesis leads to a successful therapeutic — who owns the discovery? Patent law, academic authorship norms, and database licensing agreements were not designed for this scenario. The field has no established attribution framework for AI-generated scientific discoveries, and Gemini for Science's launch makes this an urgent practical problem rather than a theoretical one.

Read More — Google Blog

FDA "Breakthrough" Device Designation Is Being Diluted by AI

The FDA's Breakthrough Device Designation was created for genuinely novel technologies with no adequate alternatives. STAT News documents AI-enabled devices applying for breakthrough status based primarily on AI integration rather than clinical superiority over existing care. As AI saturates medical devices, if everything is "breakthrough," clinicians and payers lose the navigational signal the designation was designed to provide. The FDA has not updated the eligibility criteria to reflect AI's ubiquity.

Read More — STAT News

AI-Compressed Clinical Trials Could Accelerate Harms, Not Just Approvals

The FDA's vision of AI reducing trial time by 30–40% is presented as an efficiency story. It is also a safety story — and not in the way the agency is emphasising. Clinical trial duration exists partly to surface adverse events that appear weeks or months after treatment begins. The rarest, most serious events — those that have historically triggered post-market withdrawals — are precisely those that require time to surface. The FDA's RFI should explicitly grapple with the safety case against trial compression, not only the efficiency case for it.

Read More — Government Executive

80% of Physicians Use AI Professionally — Almost None Were Formally Trained on It

Medical school curricula have not been updated to include AI literacy, bias recognition, failure mode awareness, or appropriate reliance calibration as standard competencies. The result is a generation of self-taught physician AI users applying clinical tools without a systematic understanding of when they fail, which populations they underperform on, or how to recognise AI-generated errors in clinical context. Rapid adoption without structured training creates a systemic calibration failure across the physician workforce — and the gap is widening every month.

Closing Note

When Google launches a platform that generates hypotheses, debates them with AI adversaries, and validates them against 30 curated databases — validated by papers in Nature — the question of what a scientist does has subtly shifted. Not eliminated. Not replaced. But shifted.

This week also gave us the FDA quietly embedding AI in its own regulatory reviews, 80% of physicians using AI without formal training, and a fundamental unresolved question about who owns what AI discovers. The infrastructure is being built faster than the norms that should govern it.

The most important work in AI right now may not be building better models — it may be building better frameworks for what happens when the models work.
