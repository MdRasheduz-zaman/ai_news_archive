Issue #31  |  Week of May 13, 2026

Hi, there! This week's theme is AI Drug Discovery Goes Industrial. Isomorphic Labs closed a $2.1 billion Series B — the largest fundraise in the history of AI-native drug discovery. MIT and Recursion released Boltz-2, which predicts drug binding affinity 1,000× faster than physics-based methods. A new international benchmark is stress-testing AI pathology tools against real specialists. And Nature has named the seven systemic failure modes haunting AI in medicine. Let's get into it.

🚀 EVENT OF THE WEEK

Isomorphic Labs Raises $2.1 Billion — AI Drug Discovery's Largest-Ever Fundraise

On May 12, 2026, Isomorphic Labs — the Google DeepMind spinout behind AlphaFold — announced a $2.1 billion Series B, led by Thrive Capital with participation from Alphabet, GV, MGX (Abu Dhabi), Temasek, CapitalG, and the UK Sovereign AI Fund. This is the single largest fundraise in the history of AI-native drug discovery.

The capital scales IsoDDE (Isomorphic Drug Design Engine), pushes AI-designed therapeutics toward the clinic (first trials expected by end of 2026), and deepens partnerships with Novartis, Eli Lilly, and Johnson & Johnson.

Isomorphic's proposition is that AlphaFold-class AI can be extended from structure prediction to the full drug design cycle — from target identification through molecular design to binding affinity estimation and clinical prediction. The $2.1B raise signals that institutional capital has validated this proposition at scale.

Why this matters: When sovereign wealth funds co-invest alongside Google in AI drug discovery, the field has crossed from research novelty to strategic national infrastructure. The pharmaceutical industry no longer has the option to watch from the sidelines.

Key takeaways:

  • Largest-ever Series B in AI drug discovery; UK and Abu Dhabi sovereign funds both investing signals national strategic priority
  • First clinical trials of Isomorphic-designed drugs expected before end of 2026
  • IsoDDE targets the full drug design pipeline — not just one stage

📚 Top Research Papers

DALPHIN: Benchmarking Digital Pathology AI Copilots Against Real Pathologists

arXiv 2605.03544 | May 2026

The first large-scale open benchmark evaluating vision-language model copilots against practicing pathologists. 300 cases, 14 subspecialties, 6 healthcare centers, 6 countries — testing open-ended diagnostic reasoning rather than exam-style questions. Reveals where AI currently outperforms pathologists, where it fails, and how performance varies across specialties and clinical contexts.

Digital Pathology Benchmark

A Versatile AI Agent for Rare Disease Diagnosis and Risk Gene Prioritization

arXiv 2605.06226 | May 2026

An AI agent achieving 12%–60% improvement over physicians in rare disease diagnosis accuracy, validated with clinical experts. Integrates multi-step reasoning, literature retrieval, and genomic knowledge bases to handle rare disease complexity. Rare diseases affect 300 million people globally and carry average diagnostic delays of 4–6 years.

Rare Disease Genomics

Boltz-2: Towards Accurate and Efficient Binding Affinity Prediction

arXiv 2603.05532 | MIT Jameel Clinic + Recursion Pharmaceuticals

The first deep learning model to approach FEP-level binding affinity accuracy at 1,000× the speed. Introduces experimental method conditioning, distance constraints, and multi-chain template integration. Completes predictions in ~20 seconds — enabling large-scale binding affinity screening previously possible only for large pharma with HPC infrastructure.

Drug Discovery Binding Affinity

Computational Pathology in the Era of Agentic AI — International Expert Perspectives

arXiv 2603.05884 | March 2026 — Multi-institution expert consensus

International expert consensus reviewing clinical integration readiness of pathology foundation models (UNI, CONCH, TITAN) and agentic AI. Analyses technical, regulatory, and practical barriers and proposes evaluation criteria for clinical readiness — serving as the field's roadmap from benchmark performance to actual patient benefit.

💻 Top GitHub Repos of the Week

OpenFold

⭐ ~3,200 stars | Columbia University | Python

A fully trainable, open-source AlphaFold2 implementation. Unlike DeepMind's release, OpenFold can be fine-tuned on custom protein families and therapeutic targets — critical for labs needing institution-specific predictions where the public server underperforms.

ProteinMPNN

⭐ ~2,500 stars | Baker Lab (UW) | Python

Neural protein sequence design tool — designs amino acid sequences for a given backbone structure. Core tool powering de novo protein design, antibody optimisation, and enzyme engineering pipelines now entering clinical trials.

PyTorch Geometric (PyG)

⭐ ~21,000 stars | 500+ contributors | Python

The backbone graph ML library powering most drug-target interaction models, molecular property predictors, and protein interaction networks. The infrastructure behind tools like chemprop and graphein — and directly enabling the graph-based molecular AI being scaled by companies like Isomorphic Labs.

DNABERT-2

⭐ ~600 stars | MAGICS Lab | Python

Multi-species DNA foundation model trained on 135 species genomes using Byte Pair Encoding, enabling transfer learning for promoter prediction, splice site identification, enhancer detection, and mutation effect analysis. Accessible genomic foundation model for computational biology research.

ProTrek

⭐ ~1,000 stars | Westlake University | Python

Tri-modal protein model jointly learning sequence, structure, and function — enabling cross-modal retrieval (finding proteins matching a text description of function or a structural motif). Directly useful for drug target identification where researchers need proteins matching desired functional or structural properties.

⚠️ AI Criticism & Concerns

Critical Perspectives on AI Ethics and Safety

"Seven Deadly Sins of AI in Medicine" — Nature Names the Field's Systemic Failure Modes

A survey of 914 global stakeholders in npj Digital Medicine identified seven recurring failure modes: Blind Trust, Overregulation, Dehumanization, Misaligned Optimization, Overinforming and False Forecasting, Misapplied Statistics, and Self-Referential Evaluation. Cross-cultural agreement on ethical concern — but persistent divides on how aggressively to regulate. The authors propose seven "cardinal virtues" as actionable remedies.

Read More — npj Digital Medicine

94% of AI Medical Studies Lack RCT Validation

A quantitative analysis in npj Digital Medicine finds only 6% of AI healthcare studies use randomized controlled trials — medicine's evidence gold standard. The vast majority rely on retrospective benchmarks that would not be accepted for drug or surgical approvals. The evidentiary gap between AI benchmark performance and real patient outcomes remains largely unaddressed as clinical deployment accelerates.

Read More — npj Digital Medicine

41% of Top AI Agents Have Dangerous Security Vulnerabilities

A systematic OWASP ASI audit found 41% of widely-deployed AI agents carry security flaws — prompt injection risks, insecure tool execution, data exfiltration pathways, and privilege escalation issues. For healthcare AI agents handling EHRs or clinical decisions, these vulnerabilities introduce direct patient safety risks that the field has largely not addressed.

Read More — ClawSecure / OWASP ASI

Colorado AI Act Enforcement June 30 — Healthcare AI Faces the First US Mandatory Deadline

Colorado's mandatory healthcare AI compliance requirements (disclosure, annual bias audits, anti-bias controls, 3-year records, human override mechanisms) take effect June 30, 2026. Most healthcare AI deployments are not yet compliant. This is the leading edge of what is likely to become federal requirements within 24 months — and healthcare organisations that ignore it face enforcement risk immediately.

Closing Note

The $2.1B raised by Isomorphic Labs this week is not just a funding number — it is a declaration that AI drug discovery has left the "interesting research" phase and entered the "industrial capital allocation" phase. When sovereign wealth funds from the UK and Abu Dhabi co-invest alongside Google, the thesis has been validated beyond reasonable doubt.

But this week also gave us Nature's "Seven Deadly Sins of AI in Medicine," evidence that 41% of AI agents are insecure, and a study showing 94% of clinical AI has never been rigorously validated. The industrial acceleration and the epistemic caution are not in conflict — they are the two sides of the same story, and holding both simultaneously is the most honest way to follow this field.
